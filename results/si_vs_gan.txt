
  SILICON MOSFET vs GaN HEMT -- same buck converter
  100 V -> 50 V, 500 kHz, 10 ohm load. sim/buck.cir, identical
  except the device model and its rated gate drive.
  Rds(on) matched: 25.0 mOhm GaN / 24.0 mOhm Si.
  --------------------------------------------------------------------
                  P_in     P_out      loss      eff     V_out
  GaN HEMT     241.04 W   234.82 W     6.22 W   97.42 %    48.45 V
  Si MOSFET    255.85 W   243.22 W    12.63 W   95.06 %    49.31 V
  --------------------------------------------------------------------
  GaN wastes 6.41 W less: 6.22 W against 12.63 W, a 51 % reduction
  Efficiency 97.42 % against 95.06 %, worth 2.36 points
  Loss per watt delivered: 2.65 % against 5.19 %

  NOTE, and say this if asked: the two do NOT deliver identical
  output power, because GaN's third-quadrant drop during dead time
  is larger than a silicon body-diode drop -- GaN has no body diode,
  so reverse conduction costs Vth + |Voff| + I*Rds(on) instead of one
  diode drop. That is a genuine GaN disadvantage and it is why V_out
  differs. Efficiency is a ratio so it stays a fair comparison, and
  'loss per watt delivered' above normalises it explicitly.
