BASE PAPER — how to get it
================================================================================

H. Takayama, T. Okuda and T. Hikihara,
"Digital Active Gate Drive of SiC MOSFETs for Controlling Switching Behavior
 — Preparation Toward Universal Digitization of Power Switching,"
International Journal of Circuit Theory and Applications,
vol. 50, no. 1, pp. 183–196, 2022.
DOI: 10.1002/cta.3136
https://doi.org/10.1002/cta.3136

Authors are at the Department of Electrical Engineering, Kyoto University.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
I COULD NOT DOWNLOAD IT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

It is a Wiley journal article and it is behind a paywall. Wiley also blocks
automated fetching, so there is no copy in this folder. I did not look for one
on a pirate mirror.

Three legitimate ways to get the PDF:

  1.  VIT LIBRARY.  VIT subscribes to Wiley Online Library. From the campus
      network, or through the library's off-campus / remote access portal,
      open https://doi.org/10.1002/cta.3136 and the PDF button will work.
      This is the fastest route and almost certainly works.

  2.  ASK THE AUTHORS.  Emailing a corresponding author for a copy of their
      own paper is normal and usually answered within a day or two.
      Prof. Takashi Hikihara, Dept. of Electrical Engineering, Kyoto
      University.

  3.  KYOTO UNIVERSITY REPOSITORY (KURENAI).  Japanese universities deposit
      accepted manuscripts. Search repository.kulib.kyoto-u.ac.jp for the
      title. Their pages are JavaScript-driven so I could not read them from
      here, but it is worth two minutes.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT WE TOOK FROM IT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Their contribution: an active gate driver built like a digital-to-analogue
converter, where a multibit gate code SEQUENCE sets the gate waveform. The
switching behaviour is chosen by a code instead of by a resistor.

That multibit code is the direct ancestor of our 720-point control word. The
differences are stated on the "We implemented the base paper, then ours" slide
in the backup deck:

  - theirs steps the code DURING the switching edge; ours holds it constant
  - theirs has no Miller clamp and no negative off-bias rail; both are ours
  - we implemented their driver as models/basedrv.lib and ran it in our own
    testbench, so the comparison is like for like

  Base paper, as we built it        +0.534 V   clears the 1.4 V threshold
  Ours, constant code, no clamp     -0.249 V   FALSE TURN-ON
  Ours, clamp on                    +0.570 V
  Ours, clamp + -2 V off-bias       +2.576 V   4.8x their margin

Reproduce: python3 scripts/basepaper_compare.py
