BASE PAPER
================================================================================

H. Takayama, T. Okuda and T. Hikihara,
"Digital Active Gate Drive of SiC MOSFETs for Controlling Switching Behavior
 — Preparation Toward Universal Digitization of Power Switching,"
International Journal of Circuit Theory and Applications,
vol. 50, no. 1, pp. 183–196, 2022.   DOI: 10.1002/cta.3136

Department of Electrical Engineering, Kyoto University.

  PDF: Takayama_Okuda_Hikihara_2022_Digital_Active_Gate_Drive.pdf  (12 pages)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THEY DID
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A gate driver built like a digital-to-analogue converter. The gate-source
waveform is produced by a multibit gate signal SEQUENCE rather than fixed by a
resistor, so switching behaviour is chosen by a code. They investigate which
sequences work by looking at the switching trajectory in the device's state
space, and they show experimentally that it suppresses turn-off surge voltage
and ringing.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT WE TOOK, AND WHERE WE DIFFER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Taken: the multibit gate code. It is the direct ancestor of our 720-point
control word.

Differences, all three checked against the paper itself:

  1.  THEIR DEVICE IS SiC, OURS IS GaN.
      Their paper is about SiC MOSFETs. We carry the architecture to GaN
      HEMTs, where the same idea has more to do: a GaN gate turns on at
      1.4 V against roughly 2-4 V for SiC, so there is far less margin, and
      GaN has no body diode, so the off-state gate has to be held down across
      the whole dead time. The mechanism is device-independent; the problem it
      has to solve is worse in GaN.

  2.  THEY HAVE NO MILLER CLAMP.
      The word "clamp" does not appear anywhere in the paper. Ours is a
      separately-timed active clamp.

  3.  THEY HAVE NO NEGATIVE OFF-BIAS RAIL.
      No negative gate bias is discussed. Ours selects 0 V or -2 V.

They are aware of the fault: the introduction names "self turn-on phenomenon"
as a drawback of fast switching, and cites Zhang et al. on crosstalk
suppression. They do not address it with a clamp or a negative rail.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MEASURED COMPARISON — same testbench, only the driver differs
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

We implemented their driver as models/basedrv.lib and ran it in our own
sim/dpt.cir, so the comparison is like for like.

  Base paper, as we built it        +0.534 V   clears the 1.4 V threshold
  Ours, constant code, no clamp     -0.249 V   FALSE TURN-ON
  Ours, clamp on                    +0.570 V
  Ours, clamp + -2 V off-bias       +2.576 V   4.8x their margin

Their idea is real and we reproduce it: their time-sequenced code alone clears
the threshold where a fast fixed code does not.
